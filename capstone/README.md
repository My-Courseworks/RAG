# Capstone · Ship LogiTrack's RAG System

> **What this is.** One system, designed across all 22 modules. You do not start it at the end;
> you accrue it. Each module's design exercise produces an artifact that drops into this
> capstone's decision record. By M22 you are defending a complete architecture you have
> already made every decision for.
> **Format.** Paper-and-diagram deliverables plus the labs. Engineering artifacts, not prose.
> **Prerequisite.** None beyond the modules — the capstone is the module exercises, assembled.

---

## The brief

You are the senior AI engineer at **LogiTrack**, a freight-forwarding and customs-brokerage
operator. The company has 3,000 documents today — carrier service agreements, customs procedure
manuals per jurisdiction, tariff and rate tables, dangerous-goods rules, an ops wiki, and dated
service bulletins. Leadership wants a natural-language question-answering system over all of it,
for 400 operations and customs staff across 40 jurisdictions and 12 client accounts.

**The numbers you are held to** (full spine in [syllabus §2](../syllabus.md)):

| Constraint | Value |
|---|---|
| Corpus at pilot | 3,000 documents, ~13.5M words (~18.1M tokens) |
| Corpus at 12 months | 480,000 documents, ~3.1B words |
| Questions per day | 8,500 |
| Monthly spend ceiling | $18,000 |
| P95 end-to-end latency | ≤ 2.5 s |
| Index freshness | ≤ 15 min |
| Known-item recall@10 | ≥ 0.85 |
| Tenancy | 12 client accounts, strict isolation |
| Regimes | 40 jurisdictions, audit and deletion obligations |

**What makes it hard.** Not the model call. The corpus is hierarchically structured legal prose,
mixed with tables that defeat naive text extraction, cross-referenced across documents, dated
and versioned, permissioned per client, and it grows by a factor of 160 over a year while the
budget and the latency SLO stay fixed.

## Deliverables

Eight artifacts. Each is generated by a module's design exercise; this capstone collects them.

| # | Deliverable | Produced in | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | **Architecture decision record set** | M1, M3, M5, M9, M11, M14, M18, M22 | One ADR per irreversible decision: context, options, decision, consequences, **revert condition** |
| **C2** | **Pipeline architecture diagram** | M2, refined through M22 | Every stage, its failure modes (F1–F10), its instrumentation, and its owning component |
| **C3** | **Corpus and metadata schema** | M3, M6, M17 | Ingestion schema plus the metadata contract: every filterable field, its cardinality, and its tenancy semantics |
| **C4** | **Evaluation harness and golden set** | M1, M13 | The labelled query set (60 → 400 → 1,200), the metric definitions, the regression gate, and the adjudication process for disputed labels |
| **C5** | **Retrieval configuration specification** | M7, M8, M9, M10 | Chunking config, embedding model, index type, hybrid weights, reranker, query transformations — each with its measured justification |
| **C6** | **Generation specification** | M11, M12 | Synthesis mode, prompt structure, context assembly order and budget, temperature per task with measured variance, citation policy, abstention policy |
| **C7** | **Operations plan** | M14, M18, M19, M20 | Ingest and freshness design, cost model, latency waterfall, instrumentation plan, incident classes, drift monitoring, rollback procedure |
| **C8** | **Threat model and red-team report** | M17, M21 | Permission model plus the authorisation backstop; injection and poisoning attack results with measured success rates before and after defences |

## The marking rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Pts | What earns full marks | What fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Decision quality** | 20 | Every irreversible choice has an ADR with a stated revert condition and the metric that would trigger it | Choices presented as preferences with no reversal criterion |
| **Measured claims** | 20 | Retrieval and generation numbers come from the learner's own harness on the golden set | Quality asserted from a vendor benchmark or from intuition |
| **Cost and latency arithmetic** | 15 | Per-stage latency sums to ≤ 2.5 s; cost components sum to ≤ $18k/month; both shown as derivations with stated assumptions | A single aggregate number with no breakdown |
| **Failure-mode coverage** | 15 | Each of F1–F10 mapped to a stage, a mitigation, and a test; the taxonomy is used to structure the design | Failure modes mentioned but not mapped |
| **Tenancy and permission correctness** | 10 | Cross-client leakage is tested and shown to fail closed; the authorisation backstop is post-retrieval and non-bypassable | Isolation assumed from the filter alone |
| **Adversarial robustness** | 10 | Injection and poisoning attack success rates measured before and after defences | Defences listed but untested |
| **Communicability** | 10 | A senior engineer outside the project can restate the architecture and its top three risks from the artifacts alone | Artifacts readable only by their author |

**The capstone is failed by any of:** an untested permission boundary; a quality claim with no
measurement behind it; a budget that does not sum; or an ADR whose revert condition is
"if it doesn't work."

## The three challenges (M22's oral)

You will be asked these, and must answer from your artifacts, not from general principle:

1. **"Your 12-month ingest will cost more than the annual budget in one re-embedding. What do
   you do, and what breaks?"** — tests whether you understand the convexity of chunk overlap,
   model-change re-embedding cost, and the cache/docstore incremental path (M3, M14, M18).
2. **"A carrier agreement is revised and the old version must stop being retrievable within
   15 minutes, including from your caches. Walk me through it."** — tests supersession vs
   deletion, cache invalidation, and whether you conflate "delete" with "stop retrieving"
   (M14, M19, M20, M17).
3. **"An attacker who has read this document plants one bulletin in your corpus. What is your
   blast radius, and what stopped them?"** — tests the instruction/data boundary, ingestion-time
   sanitisation, and your measured attack success rate (M21, M17).

Then one challenge of your own choosing, from your own list of what you deferred.

## How to work this capstone honestly

The temptation is to design the system you wish you had. The capstone's value is in the
opposite: **make each decision at the moment the course gives you the information, write down
what you knew and did not know, and never revise an earlier ADR silently.** When M18 shows you
that M9's reranker does not fit the latency budget, that is not a mistake to erase — it is the
ledger entry that makes you a senior engineer. Amend with a dated supersession note; keep the
original.

**Deliberately not in scope:** multi-modal retrieval, fine-tuned generators, and online learning
from user feedback beyond the golden-set loop. Their absence is a decision recorded in
[M22](../modules/22-architecture-review/README.md), not an oversight.
